Why this matters

Most breaches are not clever.
They are unattended.

The incidents we get called into are rarely exotic. They are a firewall rule nobody reviewed, a laptop nobody had on the inventory, a backup nobody had restored, or an alert that arrived at 2am and sat in an inbox until Monday. Each one is preventable, and each one is a job somebody has to actually own.

01

Policy that rots

Rules get added under pressure and never removed. After a few years nobody can say what half of them are for, or what they now allow through.

02

Devices nobody counted

The machines that cause incidents are usually the ones missing from the inventory — a contractor's laptop, a forgotten server, a phone with company mail on it.

03

Backups nobody tested

A backup that has never been restored is a hope, not a plan. The first restore should not be the one happening during an incident.

04

Alerts nobody read

Monitoring that emails a queue is not monitoring. Someone has to be awake, looking, and authorised to act on what they find.

What happens in the first hour

The value of a security partner is decided in the first hour, so here is the sequence, in order, before you ever need it.

  1. You reach a person One number, answered by an engineer who can act — not a ticket queue and not a callback promise.
  2. We contain it Isolate the affected devices and accounts and cut the path the attacker is using, before anything else. Stopping the spread comes ahead of understanding it.
  3. We work out what happened What got in, how far it reached, and what it touched — from logs and endpoint data rather than guesswork.
  4. We get you running Restore from a backup we have already tested, on the recovery time we agreed with you up front.
  5. We write it down and close the hole A plain report of what happened and what we changed, plus the fix that stops the same route being used twice.
